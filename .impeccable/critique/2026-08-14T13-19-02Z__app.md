## Design Specificity Verdict

Repbook feels authored as a calm paper training journal rather than a generic AI fitness dashboard. The warm paper field, ruled dividers, serif movement names, and restrained burnt orange reinforce the product promise. The core interaction is still partially interchangeable: the home greeting, seven-day strip, routine list, and quote occupy attention before the person can train.

The deterministic detector reported no findings in `app/(tabs)/index.tsx` or `app/workout.tsx`. This is expected for React Native source; it does not assess native layout, touch semantics, or the session-state model.

## Overall Impression

The visual system has the right temperament and the workout rep entry is the strongest screen. The next improvement is behavioral clarity: make the immediate action, its save state, and its recovery path as disciplined as the typography.

## What's Working

- The Today hero makes `Start workout` unmistakable, directly supporting Open → Train.
- Workout gives the rep number the largest visual weight and uses 52 pt plus/minus controls, appropriate for a tired user.
- The Paper & Ink palette earns its restraint: accent color denotes action, completion, and current progress instead of decoration.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Home makes the user parse too much before training

**Why it matters:** The first viewport contains a greeting, profile mark, seven-day strip, routine hero, secondary routines, and a quote. A returning user sees more journal material than training intent.

**Fix:** Keep the next routine and Start workout in the first decision group. Move the quote and secondary routine list below the initial fold, and reduce the week strip to a compact completion signal.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill app/(tabs)/index.tsx`

### [P1] Closing a workout silently destroys active work

**Why it matters:** `Close workout` calls `resetWorkout()` before navigating back. An accidental thumb tap loses all recorded reps, especially harmful mid-session or after interruption.

**Fix:** Preserve a draft automatically or show a focused discard confirmation only when the session has recorded changes. Offer `Keep training` and `Discard session`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden app/workout.tsx`

### [P1] Session labels describe the wrong unit of work

**Why it matters:** `1 / 3` is displayed beside a `Done with set` button, but the action advances to the next exercise. The user cannot tell whether they are logging sets, exercises, or the whole movement prescription.

**Fix:** Label this `Exercise 1 of 3`, use `Save & rest`, and retain a visible per-exercise set record before advancing.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify app/workout.tsx`

### [P2] The completion action is not reliably thumb-pinned

**Why it matters:** The bottom action lives inside a scrolling screen. When rest information appears or text scales up, it can move below the immediate reach zone.

**Fix:** Place the primary action in a safe-area-aware fixed footer for the workout session; keep supporting context scrollable above it.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt app/workout.tsx`

### [P2] First-timers get no confidence check before starting

**Why it matters:** Today says `3 exercises · 18 min`, but never names the movements or how the routine will progress. The person commits before knowing the first task.

**Fix:** Add a single quiet preview line under the hero metadata: `Push-up · Diamond push-up · Pike push-up`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify app/(tabs)/index.tsx`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan (Confused First-Timer)

The first action is clear, but the workout immediately asks for a rep number without a visible statement of sets, exercise order, or what happens after Done. `1 / 3` and `Done with set` create an avoidable interpretation gap.

### Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)

The rep count has a live region and critical icon controls are labeled, but text roles are manually sized down to 10–11 pt in workout metadata. The session has no announced save confirmation, and a silent reset provides no accessible recovery path.

### Casey (Distracted Mobile User)

The large rep control is well targeted, but Close can erase a session and the primary button is not structurally anchored to the bottom safe area. An interruption or an accidental tap costs more than it should.

## Minor Observations

- The Today profile mark is decorative until it provides an action or status.
- The week strip conveys completion primarily through color and a small checkmark; include a text-equivalent status for assistive technology.
- `Save your set, then take your rest.` states an outcome that is not visibly confirmed.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the journal treat every set as a durable record immediately, or only save a complete session?
- Is the seven-day strip useful enough to earn first-viewport space over the exercise preview?
- Would the calmer home screen feel more confident if it started with the routine title instead of a greeting?
